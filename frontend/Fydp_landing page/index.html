Speech in, a speaking face out.

The browser, the conversation and the avatar are separate services joined by LiveKit, so any one of them can be retrained or swapped without touching the others — and you can interrupt the avatar mid-sentence.

01 — Listening

Your voice, over LiveKit

Microphone audio travels from the browser to the agent over WebRTC. A local voice detector decides when you have finished, tuned to wait through the pauses Bangla speakers make mid-sentence rather than cut them off.

02 — Conversation

Gemini Live, speech to speech

Gemini hears the Bangla audio and answers in spoken Bangla directly, so there is no separate transcription and text-to-speech chain adding delay between your question and the reply.

03 — Avatar

Alapon renders the face

The reply audio streams to Alapon, a U-Net built on SyncTalk_2D and adapted to Bangla, which paints the mouth region of the speaker frame by frame at 30 fps on a 4 GB laptop GPU.

04 — Delivery

Back to you, in sync

Frames and audio are published together on LiveKit, so the browser plays a face whose lips land on the words — about 1.5 to 2 seconds after you stop speaking.

What we found building it.

Adapting SyncTalk_2D to Bangla meant Bangla training video, a Bangla-trained lip-sync expert and streaming audio features. Along the way, three results changed how we measured the whole field.

01 — The fix

The mouth was copying, not listening

The original mouth mask left the jaw visible, so the model copied mouth movement from the input video instead of following the audio: it moved in 41% of silent frames. Covering the jaw brings Alapon to 0.1%.

02 — The metric

Scoring above a real human

We scored real video as if it were a system. Both Wav2Lip models rate as better synced than the real speaker — impossible — and five of six published systems moved their mouths in silence without the metric noticing.

03 — The language

It follows Bangla sounds

The real speaker opens 4.0× wider on আ অ than on প ব ম; Alapon opens 3.8×, the closest of every system. Driven by six Bangla voices it had never heard, it reaches 2.3×, up from 1.7× before the fix.

Measured against six published systems.

We quoted nobody: every system was re-run by us on the same Bangla clip with the same scoring code, and real video is scored alongside them as the target. The goal is not the highest score — it is landing closest to real.

Where the project stands.

Alapon is person-specific — one face per model — and we measure how far the mouth opens, not yet its exact shape. Formal evaluation so far is on one Bangla speaker.

  1. Complete

    Real-time Bangla avatar

    Browser to Gemini to Alapon and back, running live on a consumer laptop and answering within 1.5–2 seconds.

  2. Complete

    Cross-system benchmark

    78 measurements across six systems on Bangla video and the English HDTF set, with real video scored as the reference. Being prepared as a journal paper.

  3. Next

    Retrain and human study

    A clean retrain without test-clip overlap, more Bangla speakers, and a blind, randomised study with native speakers — then a pilot in a school or public office.
